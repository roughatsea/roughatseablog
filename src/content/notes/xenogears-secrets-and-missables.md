# The Secrets of Xenogears: Hidden Items, Sidequests, Easter Eggs, and Missables

**This article contains spoilers for the entire game.**

*Xenogears* is unusually good at hiding valuable things behind behavior that no sensible player would think to repeat.

Jump down a village well again. And again. Beat a man at rock-paper-scissors five times in a row. Catch a spider and keep it for several hours. Refuse to attack a child during a martial-arts tournament. Pay Big Joe 5,000G when every instinct says he is obviously stealing your money. Fish in a fountain. Buy 20,000G worth of birds.

Then there are the nastier secrets: rewards tied to places that disappear forever, one-shot boss conditions, optional scenes buried in endgame areas, and an unused music video sitting on Disc 2 where normal gameplay never calls it.

The result is a game in which two people can finish the same 60-hour story and discover surprisingly different amounts of stuff.

This is my attempt to put the meaningful secrets in one place, with enough sourcing that a suspicious reader can check the details instead of trusting my memory of a famously enormous JRPG.

## A note about scope

There is no official Square list titled **Every Secret in Xenogears**. The boundaries are fuzzy. Does an Aquasol behind a barrel count? What about funny NPC dialogue? A rare monster drop? An unused FMV? A room that contains nothing except a visual gag?

So I am using a practical definition: a **secret** here is hidden or optional content that produces a unique or unusually useful reward, opens a sidequest, reveals a substantial Easter egg or scene, unlocks something in a minigame, or is easy to lose permanently because the story closes the area.

I am **not** claiming to catalog every ordinary chest, every minor NPC line, every sign in Lahan, or every common monster drop. Bakuto's modern [Missable Item Guide][bakuto-intro] makes a similar distinction: it deliberately covers important missables rather than every item in the game, and notes that many rare endgame items remain obtainable. I used that guide as the backbone for the one-way-item checklist, then cross-checked it against full walkthroughs by [Shotgunnova][shotgunnova], [MKnight][mknight], [Masamune167][masamune], and the old [RPGClassics Xenogears Shrine][rpg-secrets].

Where those sources disagree, I say so.

For missability, I use four plain-English labels:

- **Permanent:** once the relevant story window closes, that reward is gone for the rest of the save.
- **Route missable:** the early method or scene disappears, but the item itself has another source later.
- **Endgame-open:** optional, but still available during the final free-roam portion of Disc 2.
- **External/debug:** present on the game discs or in developer rooms, but not unlocked through ordinary play.

With that settled, we can begin in the most *Xenogears* place possible: a quiet village where half the junk in your pockets turns out to matter fifty hours later.

## Lahan: the game starts hiding endgame sidequests immediately

### The Mermaid Tear

Go to the Lahan tavern and speak to the troubled man. Choose the response that tells him **"Drinking won't help matters."** He gives Fei the **Mermaid Tear**. ([Bakuto — Lahan][bakuto-lahan]; [RPGClassics — Lahan][rpg-lahan])

The item then sits in your inventory for most of the game doing apparently nothing.

Much later, after bringing Emeralda through the Lighthouse sidequest and triggering her adult form, return to the Snowfield Hideout with her in the party. An NPC in the southeastern branch will trade the Mermaid Tear for the **Mermaid Ring**. Multiple walkthroughs agree that adult Emeralda is the condition for completing the trade. ([Ziggyshack][ziggy]; [Masamune167][masamune])

**Missability: permanent, very high.** Lahan does not remain available after the opening sequence. If you leave without the Mermaid Tear, the late-game Mermaid Ring trade never starts.

### The RPS Badge

In the southeastern house, challenge the rock-paper-scissors player and beat him **five consecutive times**. The reward is the **RPS Badge**. ([Bakuto — Lahan][bakuto-lahan]; [RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets])

This sounds like a joke item because, at this point, it effectively is one. It is actually the first piece of a three-badge sidequest that stretches from Lahan to Bledavik to Billy's orphanage, then pays off in Shevat.

RPGClassics documents a small behavioral pattern in the opponent's choices that can improve your odds, although luck remains involved. The important part is simpler: do not decide you will come back later. There is no later.

**Missability: permanent, very high.** Lose the RPS Badge and you lose the complete three-badge trade in Shevat.

### The well that rewards stubbornness

Keep jumping into the central well. Repeated drops produce an **Eyeball**, an **Aquasol S**, and eventually a **Power Ring**. ([MKnight][mknight]; [RPGClassics — Lahan][rpg-lahan])

This is a perfect early demonstration of the game's secret-design philosophy. Nothing about the first jump suggests that additional jumps will eventually cough up equipment.

**Missability: permanent, low consequence.** The Power Ring is useful early but not unique at endgame.

### The Spider Web in Fei's house

Before leaving Fei's house, rotate the camera in the side room and inspect the hidden spot for a **Spider Web**. RPGClassics lists it among Lahan's items. ([RPGClassics — Lahan][rpg-lahan])

Do not confuse this with the **Spider** on the Mountain Path. They are different inventory objects. The Spider later trades to Chu-Chu for an Ether Veiler; the Spider Web can be used as fishing line for the Dark Rock in Shevat, although Shevat also provides another way to obtain line. ([RPGClassics — Items][rpg-items]; [RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets])

**Missability: permanent as an early collectible; not required for the Shevat rock quest.**

### Lucca is teaching the tutorial

Walk into Lahan's beginner/information house and you meet **Lucca from *Chrono Trigger***. She explains save points and the Memory Cube. One old walkthrough even notes the tiny insult added to the cameo: if you let her give the Memory Cube explanation, she charges Fei 28G. ([MKnight][mknight]; [RPGClassics — Lahan][rpg-lahan])

There is no item attached to this one. It is simply a Square cameo sitting in plain sight in the first village.

**Missability: permanent scene.** Lahan is one-way.

## The Mountain Path and Citan's house: a spider, a bird egg, and a ring that guides disagree about

### Catch the Spider

Near the end of the Mountain Path, inspect the tree after crossing the bridge to catch a **Spider**. Bakuto marks it as an important missable. ([Bakuto — Mountain Path][bakuto-mountain])

Keep it.

Hours later, after Margie joins the Yggdrasil and you can enter her room, talk to Chu-Chu. She trades an **Ether Veiler** for the Spider. ([Bakuto — Road to Nisan][bakuto-road-nisan]; [Shotgunnova][shotgunnova])

This is one of those secrets that feels as if it was designed specifically to punish inventory tidiness.

**Missability: permanent.** The Mountain Path and the later Chu-Chu exchange both have limited windows.

### The Bird Egg

Another tree near the Mountain Path save point contains a **Bird Egg**. Taking it gets Fei pecked by the mother bird. If you carry the egg into Citan's house under the normal sequence of events, Yui cooks it; Shotgunnova's walkthrough records the resulting gag that Fei gains **22 pounds**. ([Shotgunnova][shotgunnova])

Shotgunnova also documents a narrow timing trick for people who want the Bird Egg itself to remain in inventory: first reach Citan's house and advance the conversation far enough to learn that Citan is in the shed, then backtrack down the slope and take the egg before continuing. This is collector behavior, not a useful combat secret.

**Missability: permanent, almost entirely for comedy/completionism.**

### Midori's Ring

Behind Citan's house, search the flower bed for **Midori's Ring**. ([Bakuto — House on the Hill][bakuto-house])

The late-game Snowfield Hideout contains the payoff: Midori trades the ring for a **Hercules Ring**. ([MKnight][mknight]; [RPGClassics — Full Walkthrough][rpg-full])

This is also a good example of why old *Xenogears* missable lists should not be copied blindly. Bakuto's progressive checklist tells you to take Midori's Ring on the first visit. MKnight explicitly says that on Disc 2 you can return to Citan's house and collect it if you did not do so earlier. I therefore would **not** call the ring permanently missable during the opening Citan sequence.

**Missability: route missable early; recoverable on Disc 2 according to full walkthroughs.**

## Bledavik: the badge quest and the fight you win by refusing to fight

### The Hide-and-Seek Badge

In Bledavik, find the boy hiding in the alley near the poorer children and accept his hide-and-seek challenge. Complete his full sequence of hiding places around town to earn the **H&S Badge**. RPGClassics maps the sequence through the northern gate, barrels near the Ethos shop, the doorway south of the restaurant, and the final corner near the town entrance. ([RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets])

Bakuto also flags the badge before the Shakhan Square progression. ([Bakuto — Operation Aveh][bakuto-aveh])

This is badge number two.

**Missability: permanent.** Finish it during the early Bledavik window.

### Dan's Wedding Dress

During the martial-arts tournament, eventually Fei is paired against Dan.

The hidden solution is to do essentially nothing hostile. **Do not attack Dan.** Wait him out. He eventually abandons the fight and leaves behind the **Wedding Dress**. ([Bakuto — Tournament][bakuto-tournament])

If Fei attacks and ends the battle normally, the reward is gone.

**Missability: permanent, very high.** This is a one-battle condition. Save before the tournament sequence if you care about unique items.

### The Bledavik fair

The fairgrounds contain several minigames and temporary festival content. Shotgunnova's sidequest notes say the fair is playable before the Fatima Castle infiltration and later disappears under the story's changing state of Bledavik. His own guide also admits that this particular fair section was not fully documented. ([Shotgunnova][shotgunnova])

That source boundary matters. There are old message-board prize lists for individual games, but they are less consistent than the documentation for the badge, Wedding Dress, or Thames card game. I would treat the fair as **temporary flavor and minor-prize content**, not as part of the essential one-of-a-kind checklist.

**Missability: permanent as an activity; low completionist consequence compared with the items above.**

## Nisan: buy the Ether Doubler while the game still lets you

The Nisan equipment shop sells the **Ether Doubler**, the accessory that doubles ether output and becomes absurdly good on the right characters and Gears.

The problem is that the shop inventory changes later. Shotgunnova warns that Ether Doublers stop being sold as the story advances, and a long-running GameFAQs FAQ places the effective deadline before the first Shevat visit / Babel Tower progression. ([Shotgunnova][shotgunnova]; [GameFAQs FAQ][gf-faq])

Bakuto marks it as a missable purchase and gives the price as 38,000G. ([Bakuto — City of Peace][bakuto-nisan])

You can find another Ether Doubler much later in the second Anima Relic dungeon, so the *item type* is not unique. The Nisan shop is the important chance to obtain extras early.

**Missability: permanent shop inventory, high gameplay consequence.** Buy at least one before Babel Tower if you want the classic ether-heavy builds.

### Holy Pendants have a hidden Gear effect

Nisan also sells **Holy Pendants**. Their normal description concerns spell duration, but the old RPGClassics secrets guide records a Gear interaction: a Holy Pendant doubles Hyper Mode duration from three turns to six. ([RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets])

This is a mechanical secret rather than a one-time sidequest. It becomes especially relevant when preparing for Hammer's Trader Card condition later.

**Missability: the easy purchase window is limited; the mechanic itself is not a one-time unlock.**

## Nortune: the game hides useful things inside an already miserable prison arc

### The sewer cache

Bakuto's missable guide flags four valuable items during the Nortune sewer sequence:

- **Cool Shades** and **Poison Gards** in the second sewer section
- **Gallant Belt** in the third section
- an **Ether Veiler** in the pipe room that requires platforming/jumping to reach

([Bakuto — Sewer Horror][bakuto-sewer])

The sewer is a story dungeon. Once the arc moves on, you do not simply stroll back in and clean up the missed chests.

**Missability: permanent area, moderate consequence.**

### Power Magic in the Gear Dock

During the Gear Dock raid, search the basement area around the crane, hook, and crate puzzle for **Power Magic**. ([Bakuto — Gear Dock Raid][bakuto-geardock])

Power Magic is one of the accessories behind *Xenogears'* famously broken ether-Gear setups, so this tiny chest has more practical value than its location suggests.

**Missability: permanent.**

### Rico's Momento Chain

When control switches to Rico in the Gear Dock sequence, inspect the dresser/mirror area in the bedroom immediately for the **Momento Chain**. ([Bakuto — Gear Dock Raid][bakuto-geardock])

This is one of the nastiest kinds of JRPG missable: a unique object hidden inside a brief character-control window while the player is thinking about the plot.

**Missability: permanent, very high.**

### Pay Big Joe the 5,000G

In the eastern civilian district, Big Joe asks for 5,000G. Pay him. ([Bakuto — Gear Dock Raid][bakuto-geardock])

Later, after the story changes Nortune during the Night Purge sequence, return to the same area and collect the **M Disk**. ([Bakuto — Night Purge][bakuto-nightpurge])

The M Disk works with jukeboxes and lets you play music. The funny part is that the early Big Joe route is not the only way to own one: MKnight's late-game walkthrough says an M Disk can also be won through Disc 2 arena battling. ([MKnight][mknight])

**Missability: route missable.** The Big Joe event is gone if skipped, but the M Disk itself has a later source.

### Monster parts are worth more if you wait

Nortune contains another underexplained system. RPGClassics recommends holding onto monster parts until Rico earns the **Rank S** title because Nortune's exchange values improve, and notes that Disc 2 Nortune has a central computer used to trade those parts. ([RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets])

This is not a unique-item secret so much as an economy secret. Selling every Fang, Scale, and similar drop at the first merchant can mean throwing away a lot of money.

**Missability: no. It is a hidden optimization, not a one-shot reward.**

## Goliath Factory: an O2 Cylinder and a secret arena roster condition

Bakuto flags an **O2 Cylinder** shortly after the first factory save point: hug the left wall and collect it before committing to the conveyor-belt progression. ([Bakuto — Secret Weapon][bakuto-goliath])

**Missability: permanent.** Goliath Factory is a one-way story area.

There is also a stranger completionist wrinkle. Dan_GC's old **Battling FAQ** reports that encountering the rare purple **Shinobi** Gear in Goliath Factory unlocks Shinobi as a selectable Gear in the arena roster. ([Dan_GC — Battling FAQ][battling]) Because the factory cannot be revisited, an arena completionist should make sure that encounter happens before leaving.

I am putting a little more distance around this claim than around the O2 Cylinder: it is documented in the specialist arena FAQ rather than the modern missable guide.

**Missability: permanent arena-roster condition, according to the arena FAQ.**

## Thames: a card game quietly holds four unique rewards

The Thames card game usually gets called **Speed** in old guides. You place cards in numerical sequence onto the two center piles and race the opponent to empty your hand. Character movement speed matters; guides commonly recommend Citan or Emeralda. ([RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets])

The important opponents are scattered across the Thames and, later, the Yggdrasil:

| Opponent | Location | Reward | Window |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kid | Supply Entrance | **Super Goggles** | Before Thames story progression |
| Man | Beer Hall | **Premier Shoes** | Before Thames story progression |
| Queenie | Armor Ground | **Death Blower 1** | Before Thames story progression |
| Big Joe | Yggdrasil top deck | **Long Dark Coat** | Brief later Yggdrasil window |

RPGClassics and Bakuto independently document these rewards, while Bakuto also flags a **D Circuit** hidden in the Supply Entrance area. ([RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets]; [Bakuto — Man of the Sea][bakuto-thames]; [Bakuto — Betrayal][bakuto-betrayal])

The Big Joe match is particularly easy to miss because it appears during a narrow stretch of story aboard the Yggdrasil rather than in the obvious minigame hub.

**Missability: permanent for the Disc 1 opponent rewards.**

The game revives Speed in the Snowfield Hideout on Disc 2, where Kanna offers character dolls as prizes. RPGClassics lists **Emer Jr. Doll** for one win and **Elly Jr. Doll** for five consecutive wins; it also lists an intermediate Bart Jr. Doll result. Older walkthroughs are not perfectly consistent about the intermediate reward, so I would trust the one-win and five-win milestones first if collecting. ([RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets]; [RPGClassics — Full Walkthrough][rpg-full])

**Missability: endgame-open.**

## Billy's orphanage: three books, a chicken corral, and a hidden gun

The orphanage hides an entire little chain behind children who do not look like quest-givers.

### Book 1

Talk to the child in the upper area and agree to keep the secret. You receive **Book 1**. ([Bakuto — Orphanage][bakuto-orphanage])

### Book 2

At the bathroom, keep trying to enter. Eventually the child involved gives you **Book 2**. ([Bakuto — Orphanage][bakuto-orphanage])

### Book 3

Bring **Rico** in the party and talk to the relevant child near Primera's room; the sequence involving the Bell Amulet produces **Book 3**. ([Bakuto — Orphanage][bakuto-orphanage])

### The Tag Badge

Go to the chicken pen and help the child corral the chickens. The **Tag Badge** is the reward. RPGClassics describes pressing the interaction button to make a chicken follow, repeating when it wanders off. ([RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets]; [Bakuto — Orphanage][bakuto-orphanage])

That completes the badge trio:

**RPS Badge + H&S Badge + Tag Badge.**

### The B&J M686 gun

Return with all three books. Use them at the orphanage bookcase to expose the hidden compartment and collect the **B&J M686**, one of Billy's guns. ([Bakuto — Burning Souls][bakuto-burning])

The orphanage eventually becomes unavailable in this form, so this entire chain deserves to be handled before advancing the Ethos/Zeboim story past it.

**Missability: permanent.** The Tag Badge also controls the later Shevat badge reward.

## Babel Tower: one chest is deliberately placed where falling looks like failure

After activating the computers in Babel Tower, Bakuto directs the player to **drop down the shaft** to reach the **Veil Doubler** before proceeding to the repair bot. ([Bakuto — Babel Tower][bakuto-babel])

This is another recurring *Xenogears* trick: a platforming route that visually reads like the wrong way down turns out to be the way to a unique chest.

**Missability: permanent.**

## Shevat: the richest concentration of secrets in the game

If there is one point in *Xenogears* where a completionist should stop following the story and inspect everything, it is the first visit to Shevat.

Two unrelated multi-hour secret chains terminate here, and the city itself contains a three-part rock puzzle with a hidden cache.

### Cash in the three badges

Take the **RPS Badge**, **H&S Badge**, and **Tag Badge** to the Chu-Chu area and talk to the appropriate older Chu-Chu. Bakuto records the reward as a **Charger A** and **Power Ring S**. ([Bakuto — Sky City Shevat][bakuto-shevat])

Shotgunnova says this trade remains available from the first Shevat visit until the Solaris infiltration, but not on Disc 2. ([Shotgunnova][shotgunnova])

**Missability: permanent.** The real danger is that each badge has its own earlier permanent failure point.

### Find the Dawn Rock

On the exterior Shevat map before the palace/city proper, travel toward the southwest edge and inspect the end of the narrow structure/spoke for the **Dawn Rock**. ([Bakuto — Sky City Shevat][bakuto-shevat]; [RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets])

### Find the Dusk Rock

Reach the abandoned-house area below the Aphel Aura shops—the house associated with the wooden-horse/doll scene—and inspect the doll for the **Dusk Rock**. ([Bakuto — Sky City Shevat][bakuto-shevat]; [RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets])

### Fish up the Dark Rock

First talk to the pink Chu-Chu perched high on a lamp in the Chu-Chu room. Then go to the fountain area. Obtain fishing line/thread there if you did not preserve an earlier Spider Web, climb onto the fountain, and keep fishing until you pull up the **Dark Rock**. ([Bakuto — Sky City Shevat][bakuto-shevat]; [RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets])

The exact old-guide wording alternates between buying thread and Spider Web, but both sources agree on the important conditions: talk to the lamp Chu-Chu, fish from the fountain, and keep at it until the Dark Rock appears.

### Open the three-rock cache

With all three rocks, return to the otherwise nonfunctional floating platform near the early Shevat area. It activates and carries you to a hidden room. Search the shelves and furniture.

Bakuto identifies the headline rewards as **Power Crisis**, **Yamato Belt**, and **Chu-Chu Idol**. Shotgunnova's fuller walkthrough lists additional consumables and permanent stat drives in the room. ([Bakuto — Sky City Shevat][bakuto-shevat]; [Shotgunnova][shotgunnova])

Power Crisis alone makes this one worth doing. It becomes one of the most abusable accessories in the late game because its damage bonus increases as the wearer approaches death.

**Missability: permanent, very high.** First-visit Shevat does not remain freely accessible after the story destroys/changes the city.

### The emergency-shaft items

During the later Shevat defense sequence, Bakuto also flags a **Battle Apron** and an **Economether** in side paths before the ladder exit. ([Bakuto — Intruder Alert][bakuto-intruder])

**Missability: permanent.** This is a one-way emergency sequence inside an already temporary location.

## The Gates: three Death Blowers placed where you can walk right past them

Three of the Death Blower accessories are tucked into one-way gate-related areas.

**Death Blower 1 — Nisan Mausoleum.** Before operating the Jasper Lock, search south of the lock while hugging the left wall. ([Bakuto — Protect Nisan][bakuto-protect])

**Death Blower 2 — Sargasso / Gate 3.** It sits near the door immediately before the boss. Take it before entering. ([Bakuto — Gate 3][bakuto-gate3])

**Death Blower 3 — Solaris / Krelian's Lab.** This one appears among a much larger cluster of missables described below. ([Bakuto — Krelian's Lab][bakuto-krelian])

**Missability: permanent for each one-way dungeon copy.** Death Blower accessories do have other sources in the game, so completionists should distinguish unique acquisition windows from unique item types.

## Solaris: inspect the city for Easter eggs, then strip Krelian's Lab before leaving

### The Tifa poster

In Solaris's second-class citizen area, one room contains a small Gear flying around. Look at the wall above the beds and you can see a poster of **Tifa Lockhart from *Final Fantasy VII***. RPGClassics' Solaris walkthrough calls out the room directly, and Masamune167's walkthrough independently identifies the image as a Tifa cameo. ([RPGClassics — Solaris][rpg-solaris]; [Masamune167][masamune])

The same neighborhood contains rotating Elly imagery and other visual jokes documented on the Xenogears Shrine's tidbits page. ([RPGClassics — Tidbits][rpg-tidbits])

**Missability: permanent scene/Easter egg.** Solaris is famously not a place you revisit later for tourism.

### Krelian's Lab has a small department store hidden in its side rooms

Bakuto's missable checklist identifies the following one-time items in Krelian's Lab:

- **Muscle Belt** in a room south/southeast of the Omnigear hangar
- **Angelic Robe** southwest of that area
- **Ether Veiler** and **Death Blower 3** in a small room near the save point
- **Dark Helm** near the audio-password console
- **Dark Armor** in a nearby side room
- **Extra Ar DX** after the audio-password stairway section
- **Sonic Wave** off the main hall
- **Speed Ring S** in the hologram room

([Bakuto — Krelian's Lab][bakuto-krelian])

Bakuto's practical warning is to finish the cleanup **before progressing beyond the hologram room**.

**Missability: permanent, high.** Solaris is a one-way story sequence and the lab does not become an endgame shopping trip.

## Disc 2's Anima Relic dungeons: one-way areas continue even after the game opens up

Disc 2 eventually gives the player a surprisingly open world map again, but the Anima Relic dungeons still contain missable interiors.

### First Anima Relic dungeon

Bakuto marks four items:

- **Starlight** in the northwest before the facility doors; leave the Gears to reach it
- **Ether Guard** behind the shop bot
- **King's Helm** to the west/left of the entrance
- **Wizardry Ring** in the dark-room/balcony route

Get them before completing the computer/password progression. ([Bakuto — Soul Vessel][bakuto-anima1])

**Missability: permanent.**

### Second Anima Relic dungeon

The second dungeon contains another cluster:

- **Ether Guard** in the first cavern
- **Rose Tabard** in the second cavern
- **Beastly Robe**
- **King's Helm**
- **Ether Doubler** around the water-level puzzle routes

Bakuto specifically recommends trying the water-level combinations and exploring the paths they expose before moving on. ([Bakuto — The Stars Know][bakuto-anima2])

**Missability: permanent.**

And then the dungeon gives you the most important one-shot reward in the game.

## Hammer's Trader Card: the missable that deserves its own save file

The Hammer boss fight in the second Anima Relic dungeon has an unusual victory condition.

Hammer has **48,000 HP**. After roughly 28,000 damage, he begins glowing red and enters the self-destruct sequence. If the fight ends through the self-destruct/escape outcome, you do **not** receive the **Trader Card**. Kill Hammer yourself before the explosion and the Trader Card drops. Shotgunnova, Masamune167, MKnight, Ziggyshack, and the specialist boss/monster references all agree on the basic condition. ([Shotgunnova][shotgunnova]; [Masamune167][masamune]; [Ziggyshack][ziggy])

The safest approach is to save before the relic room, turn on Booster, bring strong burst damage, build Attack Levels before crossing the critical damage threshold, and be ready to use **System Id**, level-3/infinity Gear deathblows, or a heavily boosted Aerods setup as soon as Hammer turns red. Masamune167 gives the threshold as about 28,000 damage / 20,000 HP remaining; Shotgunnova's damage accounting is slightly different, which is another reason not to try to land on the threshold with single-digit precision. ([Masamune167][masamune]; [Shotgunnova][shotgunnova])

Why care so much about one accessory?

The Trader Card forces the rare-drop side of many enemy drop tables while it is equipped. It turns late-game rare-item farming from a slot-machine exercise into something much more deterministic. ([Masamune167][masamune]; [GameFAQs Monster List][monster-list])

That feeds directly into secrets such as **Speed Shoes** from the Slugger forests around Taura's House and high-end Dragon drops on Duneman Isle. ([RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets])

**Missability: permanent, extreme.** There is one Hammer battle and one Trader Card. Make a separate save before it.

## Disc 2 opens a second game underneath the first one

After the Anima Relics and the major Disc 2 story events, *Xenogears* gives you a final free-roam period. This is where many of the game's best secrets stop being missable and become merely obscure.

Bakuto's progressive missable guide explicitly notes that, after its last Anima Relic section, the remaining important rare items are generally **not** missable. ([Bakuto — Introduction][bakuto-intro])

That does not make them easy to find.

## Snowfield Hideout: cash in things the game has been making you carry for fifty hours

### Midori's Ring becomes a Hercules Ring

Talk to Midori with **Midori's Ring** and she trades it for a **Hercules Ring**. ([RPGClassics — Full Walkthrough][rpg-full]; [MKnight][mknight])

If you skipped the ring near Citan's house early in the game, MKnight says you can return there on Disc 2 and get it now.

**Missability: endgame-open / recoverable.**

### The Kishin Sword becomes the Kijin Sword

Before speaking to the swordsmith, unequip the **Kishin Sword** from Fenrir/Citan's Gear setup. The smith upgrades it into the **Kijin Sword**. ([RPGClassics — Full Walkthrough][rpg-full]; [Atom_Edge Side Quest FAQ][atom])

The name similarity makes this secret easy to flatten into a typo when discussing it from memory. They are two different stages of the weapon.

**Missability: endgame-open.**

### Buy the ridiculously expensive birds

An NPC sells a flock of birds for **20,000G**. Buy them.

The birds fly away, revealing the lift/platform they were sitting on. Ride it up into the former Chu-Chu area and search the display cases. Guides list **Speed Shoes, Hercules Ring, Wizardry Ring, Vivid Turban, Hero Costume, and Yamato Belt** among the haul. ([GheddonLN][gheddon]; [Atom_Edge Side Quest FAQ][atom])

This may be the most expensive elevator button in PlayStation history.

**Missability: endgame-open.**

### The miner eventually uncovers a jukebox

Talk to the miner in the Snowfield Hideout and follow the Gold Nugget / Gold Bullion sequence, leaving or resting as required between stages. He eventually clears access to a jukebox. If you have the **M Disk**, you can use it there; Nortune's arena also provides music access later. ([Shotgunnova][shotgunnova]; [MKnight][mknight])

**Missability: endgame-open.**

### There is an optional Zephyr/Ramsus scene

The Snowfield Hideout also lets you revisit **Queen Zephyr** for an optional Ramsus-related scene. Full walkthroughs flag it during the endgame cleanup. ([MKnight][mknight]; [RPGClassics — Full Walkthrough][rpg-full])

No superweapon falls out of the conversation. It is simply extra character material in a game whose final disc famously compresses enormous amounts of story.

**Missability: endgame-open until the final commitment.**

## Taura's House: free armor outside, Speed Shoes in the forest

Return to Taura's House and speak with him in the nanoreactor area. He gives you the **Manly Mantle** and **Goddess Robe**. ([MKnight][mknight]; [GheddonLN][gheddon])

Then wander the forests around the house until you encounter a **Slugger**. With the Trader Card equipped, Slugger yields **Speed Shoes**. RPGClassics specifically recommends the forests around Taura's House for this farm. ([RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets])

Speed Shoes dramatically shorten the delay between turns and work in ways that make late-game character and Gear combat much faster. This is one reason the Hammer fight matters so much: the Trader Card converts a rare-drop hunt into a repeatable farm.

**Missability: endgame-open.** The Trader Card prerequisite is the permanently missable part.

---

## The Lighthouse: a lost city, adult Emeralda, and Big Joe's final act

The Lighthouse is an optional late-game area that is easy to mistake for scenery on the world map.

Bring **Emeralda**.

The elevator takes the party into the buried ruins of the Zeboim-era city. Explore the streets, buildings, and underground routes. One southern parking-lot/manhole route leads into the subway; a chest along the way contains a **Z Charger**. Continue to the balcony/terminal sequence with Emeralda and the game triggers the scene in which she takes on her adult form. ([MKnight][mknight]; [Masamune167][masamune])

The area also contains late-game equipment, environmental storytelling, computers, old media, and **Big Joe's shop**. This is the final form of a joke NPC who has somehow become one of the best merchants in the world.

After Emeralda grows up, return to the Snowfield Hideout with her and trade the Lahan **Mermaid Tear** for the **Mermaid Ring**. ([Ziggyshack][ziggy]; [Masamune167][masamune])

**Missability: endgame-open.** The permanently missable prerequisite is the Mermaid Tear from the opening village.

---

## Duneman Isle: the world-map location the game barely acknowledges

Duneman Isle is a hidden optional island in the late-game world. It does not present itself like a normal story destination. Walkthroughs place it between/near Bart's Hideout and Merkava; land on the grassy southern coast and refuse the Dune Men's request to leave. ([MKnight][mknight]; [RPGClassics — Duneman Isle][rpg-duneman])

Inside, navigate east through the desert screens and then north to the enormous skeleton. Climb the bones to claim the **Yamato Sword**. ([RPGClassics — Duneman Isle][rpg-duneman])

At the sandfall, two chests flash past on the descent. One contains **Kingly Armor** and the other the **God Father** gun. You can only grab one on a given fall, so loop back through the route to collect the other. ([SLee][slee]; [RPGClassics — Duneman Isle][rpg-duneman])

The island's **Dragon** encounters are the real endgame loot mine. Old guides list drops such as **Death Adder**, **Blessed Habit**, **Blessed Helm**, **Kaiser Helm**, and **Mumyo Sword**, depending on the drop table/source. Equip the Trader Card before farming. ([MKnight][mknight]; [RPGClassics — Duneman Isle][rpg-duneman])

**Missability: endgame-open.** Again, the Trader Card is the one thing you cannot repair if you missed it earlier.

## Nortune's Disc 2 arena has secret Gears

The Battling Arena becomes much more interesting when you return late in the game.

### Argento

Dan_GC's specialist Battling FAQ gives the most precise recipe I found:

Set **Number of Matches = 3**, **Difficulty = Hard**, and use **Player vs. Computer**. Pick one Gear and beat every other available opponent with that same Gear without losing a match. You can lose individual rounds. After clearing the roster, choose **Xenogears** and fight the Gear you used for the sweep. Completing that final fight unlocks **Argento** as a secret selectable Gear. ([Dan_GC — Battling FAQ][battling])

RPGClassics gives essentially the same unlock in shorter form. ([RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets])

**Missability: endgame-open.**

### El-Regrs / Elly's Omnigear

Enter the final dungeon, **Deus**, progress far enough to jump down into its lower section, then use the exit and return to Nortune. The arena gains **El-Regrs** (often written Regrs in old FAQs), Elly's Omnigear, as an arena selection. ([Dan_GC — Battling FAQ][battling]; [RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets])

This is a lovely little cruelty: the game waits until Elly is no longer normally available for the main endgame party, then quietly lets you drive her Omnigear in a minigame.

**Missability: endgame-open, provided you leave Deus before the final commitment.**

## The unused "Stars of Tears" music video is really on Disc 2

This one belongs in a different category from the rest because you cannot unlock it by playing normally.

The second disc contains an **unused music-video FMV** associated with the vocal track fans often call "Star of Tears." Square Enix's official soundtrack listing gives the track title as **"Stars of Tears (Outtake)."** ([Square Enix — Xenogears Original Soundtrack][square-ost])

The old RPGClassics secrets page documents the video as disc content and describes extracting/viewing it with PlayStation media tools. ([RPGClassics — Secrets][rpg-secrets]) A dedicated GameFAQs debug-room guide provides an in-game developer route: in the **Saito** debug room, use Sophia's movie mode and select the Disc 2 movie entry on page 3. ([ADean — Debug Room Guide][debug])

The distinction matters: this is not a secret ending, a hidden cutscene triggered by an obscure choice, or a reward for 100% completion. It is **unused content** that shipped on the disc.

**Missability: external/debug.** A normal save file neither earns nor loses it.

## A few things I deliberately did not pretend were settled

Old *Xenogears* documentation is excellent by late-1990s FAQ standards, but it is still old fan documentation. Some details differ among guides.

**Midori's Ring** is the clearest example. A progressive missable guide tells you to take it during the first Citan visit; full endgame walkthroughs say you can return on Disc 2 if you did not. I have marked it recoverable.

**The Snowfield Speed prizes** are another. Sources agree on the one-win Emeralda doll and the five-consecutive-win Elly doll. The exact intermediate doll reporting is less consistent, so I would not build a perfect-file plan around a single old list without testing it on the version you are playing.

**The Bledavik fair** has plenty of temporary content, but the best modern full walkthrough I found explicitly leaves its prize documentation unfinished. Rather than turn message-board fragments into certainty, I left the fair out of the essential unique-item checklist.

And I have not labeled every rare one-shot boss drop as a "secret." The item and monster FAQs contain deep collector material, including rare-drop tables whose practical attainability generated years of forum arguments. The Trader Card section gives you the important system; a literal all-drops perfect-inventory guide would be a separate article.

That is a better boundary than inventing confidence where the sources do not support it.

## The odd genius of Xenogears' secrets

A lot of 1990s JRPG secrets were obscure because developers expected players to talk to friends, buy strategy guides, or simply replay games for years.

*Xenogears* goes further because its secrets echo the structure of the story. Tiny objects acquire meaning much later. A piece of junk from Lahan survives the destruction of Lahan. A child's badge follows Fei across countries. A spider becomes equipment. Big Joe's obvious scam becomes a music disc. Midori's ring waits for the world to end. Emeralda's sidequest sends you into the ruins of the civilization that created her.

Even the mechanical secrets have this quality. Hammer's Trader Card turns his final betrayal into the key to the game's rarest loot economy. Shevat hides its best cache behind three meaningless rocks scattered through spaces the story is urging you to hurry past. The final dungeon unlocks a Gear for a character who is no longer standing beside you.

The game rewards the player who treats its world as a place rather than a corridor between cutscenes.

That is why a good *Xenogears* secrets guide cannot consist only of a list of treasure chests. The memorable secrets are the ones that make you ask, twenty hours later, **wait, that thing was for this?**
