As of August 16, 2026, Square Enix has not announced *Final Fantasy XVII*. There is no logo, trailer, release window, director, platform list, setting, protagonist, composer, combat system, or even a formal confirmation that a project called *Final Fantasy XVII* is in production.

That sounds like the end of the article.

It is not.

For several years, senior Final Fantasy developers have talked about "Final Fantasy 17" in public. Square Enix has also changed how it organizes its development teams, how it thinks about platforms, and how often it wants major franchises to appear. In 2026, Naoki Hamaguchi -- the director of the *Final Fantasy VII Remake* trilogy -- began talking about what his team might do after *Final Fantasy VII Revelation*. A month later, Naoki Yoshida publicly read out fan comments asking Hamaguchi to make *Final Fantasy XVII* and then jokingly stopped him from answering.

None of that amounts to an announcement. Taken together, however, it gives us more to work with than "we know nothing."

This is an attempt to keep the evidence, inference, and wishful thinking in separate boxes.

| Question | Best answer today |
| --- | --- |

| Confirmed in full production? | No |
| Some internal planning or preproduction likely? | Yes, in my judgment |

## The empty part of the page

The safest starting point is the long list of things we do not know.

Square Enix has disclosed nothing reliable about the story, world, protagonist, party, job system, summons, magic system, technology level, art direction, character designer, scenario writer, composer, engine, or release date. There is no credible evidence that the game is medieval, futuristic, open world, linear, turn-based, action-based, an MMO, or built around a customizable hero.

Even the title deserves a tiny asterisk. The obvious expectation is that the next numbered game will be called *Final Fantasy XVII*, and Square Enix developers themselves routinely use "17" when discussing the future. But until Square Enix announces it, the title remains an expectation rather than a product listing.

So any alleged *Final Fantasy XVII* screenshot, logo, plot synopsis, protagonist design, platform list, or engine claim should be treated as fan work, speculation, or a leak that still needs independent verification.

The useful material begins with what Square Enix's own people have said.

## Yoshida has been talking about the game since before XVI shipped

In 2022, while *Final Fantasy XVI* was still in development, Naoki Yoshida was already describing the next numbered Final Fantasy as something that should change hands.

Speaking to GamesRadar, he said the next game would have "a different team," "a different direction," "a different world," and "a different battle system." The point was broader than a prediction about one sequel. Yoshida was describing the modern identity of Final Fantasy: each numbered entry gets to decide what Final Fantasy means for itself.

He even joked about the possibility of pixel graphics while discussing the relationship between visual fidelity and command-based combat. The joke was not a secret announcement of an HD-2D *Final Fantasy XVII*. The useful part of the exchange was his insistence that the next team should not be forced to inherit XVI's exact design philosophy.

A [2023 interview with *Wired*](https://www.wired.com/story/final-fantasy-16-interview-yoshida-takai/) made the handoff more explicit. Yoshida said:

> "Whoever makes Final Fantasy XVII, it's probably not going to be us."

He framed XVI partly as a demonstration to future creators that they could take the series somewhere else. "They can create whatever they want," he said.

That does not prove that Creative Studio III -- the organization associated with *Final Fantasy XIV* and *XVI* -- is completely absent from XVII. Large Square Enix projects can pull staff across organizational boundaries, and the company has since reorganized its Japanese development structure anyway. But it is strong evidence against the assumption that XVI established a permanent "mainline Final Fantasy team" that would simply move on to XVII.

The same idea resurfaced in January 2024. During an Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences conversation with Shuhei Yoshida, Naoki Yoshida said that nothing had been decided publicly and suggested that it might be time for "someone new" to take on the series. He specifically talked about handing the next game to a younger generation with different sensibilities.

That comment is often flattened into "Final Fantasy XVII will be made by a younger team." Yoshida did not announce that. He said he thought it would be a good direction.

The distinction matters because so much of the XVII rumor cycle consists of somebody expressing a preference and the internet upgrading it into a production fact.

## The combat system is genuinely open

The largest recurring argument around XVII is whether Square Enix will continue XVI's real-time action combat or swing back toward turn-based play.

There is no announced answer.

At Anime Expo 2025, after *Clair Obscur: Expedition 33* had reignited the usual debate over whether big-budget RPGs could still succeed with turn-based systems, Yoshida was asked what that meant for Final Fantasy. His answer rejected the premise that the battle system should be chosen first.

He argued that combat has to fit the game the creators want to make: its presentation, narrative, design, and overall feel. He said there was no "clear-cut answer" between turn-based and action systems and added that he was not necessarily going to be on *Final Fantasy XVII*. He did not want to put the future directors of XVII or XVIII "on a rail."

That is one of the firmer things we have learned.

There does not appear to be a public corporate rule that says numbered Final Fantasy games must now be action games.

It also does not follow that Square Enix has decided to return to classic menu-driven turns. A traditional turn-based XVII, an action game, a *Final Fantasy VII Remake*-style hybrid, or a system we have not seen before all remain compatible with the comments we have.

My own guess leans toward some kind of hybrid, especially if Hamaguchi's team becomes involved, but that is a prediction based on the team's existing strengths. It is not evidence about XVII.

## Square Enix itself has changed since XVI

Developer interviews tell us what individual creators think. Square Enix's corporate strategy tells us what kind of production environment the next game would enter.

In 2024, the company announced a major restructuring under its medium-term plan, ["Square Enix Reboots and Awakens."](https://www.hd.square-enix.com/eng/ir/policy/message2024_4.html) The company said it would move from "quantity to quality," retire the old business-unit-centered development structure, integrate its development resources more closely, and give large projects more centralized oversight.

The same plan committed Square Enix to a multiplatform strategy for HD games so that "major franchises and AAA titles" could reach more customers.

That policy has since become visible in the Final Fantasy release schedule itself. *Final Fantasy VII Rebirth* expanded to Xbox Series X|S and Nintendo Switch 2 in 2026, and *Final Fantasy VII Revelation* is scheduled to launch across PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch 2, and PC in spring 2027.

A future numbered Final Fantasy returning to a long PlayStation-only window would now run against Square Enix's stated strategy and its recent behavior. It is still possible that hardware constraints, business deals, or the timing of a future console generation could complicate an exact day-one platform list. But broad multiplatform support is one of the safest predictions available.

The company has also been talking about cadence.

In its 2025 shareholder messaging, Square Enix said it wanted a structure that could "steadily launch major titles" centered on its core IP. The [2024 plan](https://www.hd.square-enix.com/eng/ir/policy/message2024_4.html) discussed building a lineup with better launch timing, and older investor briefings had already talked about shortening the content launch cycle. In its [May 2026 progress report](https://www.hd.square-enix.com/eng/ir/library/pdf/20260514_01_en.pdf), Square Enix said it had rebuilt its medium-to-long-term pipeline, was completing the transition of major titles to a multiplatform strategy, and was making steady progress toward "regular new title launches for major IP."

By 2026, Yoshida was publicly connecting the long gaps between Final Fantasy releases with the series' difficulty reaching younger players. His point was simple: a younger player cannot form the same relationship with a series if a new numbered entry appears only once or twice during an entire stage of their life.

That gives Square Enix a problem XVII may have to solve behind the scenes: how do you build a lavish modern Final Fantasy without making every numbered installment a seven-year event?

## Then Naoki Hamaguchi became hard to ignore

For years, talk about who might direct XVII had very little substance. In 2026, Naoki Hamaguchi finally gave the speculation something concrete to attach itself to.

Hamaguchi has spent more than a decade on the *Final Fantasy VII Remake* project. He directed *Final Fantasy VII Rebirth* and now *Final Fantasy VII Revelation*, the final game in the trilogy. Compared with figures such as Yoshinori Kitase and Tetsuya Nomura, he represents a newer generation of Square Enix leadership, even though he is hardly a newcomer; his Square Enix credits reach back to the PlayStation 2 era.

In a [June 2026 interview with *Game Informer*](https://gameinformer.com/2026/06/12/we-asked-final-fantasy-vii-revelation-director-naoki-hamaguchi-whats-next-for-him?pubDate=20260614), Hamaguchi said he expected his next creative work to be another JRPG. He wanted to take on another large-scale RPG and left the identity of that project open: Final Fantasy or another IP.

The more suggestive comment came when he discussed the future of his team. Referring to possibilities after *Revelation*, Hamaguchi said the group could work on something smaller, "a new Final Fantasy title," or a new AAA franchise.

That still leaves a lot of room. "A new Final Fantasy title" could mean XVII, XVIII, a spinoff, or a project that never leaves planning. But it is direct evidence that Hamaguchi sees another Final Fantasy as a plausible next assignment for the team he has spent years building.

The team itself is part of the reason the idea makes sense.

Hamaguchi has repeatedly talked about the value of keeping the *Remake* developers together across multiple games. Instead of assembling a giant production group, shipping one game, and scattering everybody to unrelated projects, Square Enix kept a large amount of institutional knowledge intact. The developers learned the tools, the combat language, the asset pipeline, and each other's habits over the course of a trilogy.

Square Enix has spent the last few years telling investors that it wants better use of internal talent, more efficient large-project management, and steadier releases from major IP.

A mature AAA JRPG team finishing one of the company's biggest projects fits that problem unusually well.

This is why "Hamaguchi might direct Final Fantasy XVII" has moved beyond random fan casting. It is still unconfirmed. It now has an organizational argument behind it.

## The Berlin Fan Fest moment

Then came Final Fantasy XIV Fan Festival 2026 in Berlin.

During the "Naoki's Room" panel, Yoshida read audience comments asking for Hamaguchi to make *Final Fantasy XVII*. According to [GamesRadar's report from the panel](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/final-fantasy/i-want-this-guy-to-make-final-fantasy-17-yoshi-p-embarrasses-final-fantasy-7-remake-lead-with-the-highest-praise-you-can-get-from-players-live-on-stage-at-ff14-fan-fest/), Yoshida called that about the highest praise Hamaguchi could receive.

Hamaguchi began to respond.

Yoshida stopped him: "No, no, don't!"

This is excellent rumor fuel and terrible proof.

Yoshida may simply have been rescuing Hamaguchi from an awkward question about an unannounced future. They may have been teasing an audience that they knew would overanalyze the exchange. Or Hamaguchi may actually know something about XVII that he cannot discuss.

The clip cannot tell us which explanation is correct.

What changes the weight of the moment is the timing. Only weeks earlier, Hamaguchi had publicly said his team could move on to "a new Final Fantasy title." Yoshida then brought up XVII specifically, with Hamaguchi sitting beside him, and cut off the reply.

That sequence is enough to make Hamaguchi the strongest public candidate I can identify. It is not enough to call him the director.

## The awkward timing problem

There is a practical problem with the Hamaguchi theory.

*Final Fantasy VII Revelation* is scheduled for spring 2027. Hamaguchi says he is focused on finishing it. If his entire team finishes *Revelation*, takes a breath, and only then begins a huge new numbered Final Fantasy from scratch, a release around 2032 would not be surprising.

Square Enix is simultaneously telling investors that it wants a steadier pipeline and Yoshida is lamenting the long gaps between numbered games.

Those facts do not fit comfortably together.

If Hamaguchi really is tied to XVII, one plausible production model is that a smaller group has already been doing concept work, prototyping, staffing, or preproduction while the bulk of the *Revelation* team finishes its current game. Another possibility is that Hamaguchi's team is being considered for a different future Final Fantasy and XVII belongs to somebody else entirely.

We cannot distinguish those possibilities from public information.

This is also why "Is Final Fantasy XVII in development?" needs a careful answer.

I would be surprised if Square Enix had reached 2026 without internal plans, prototypes, staffing discussions, or portfolio slots for the next numbered Final Fantasy. That is an inference from how AAA development works and from the company's own statements about managing its medium-term pipeline. It is not the same as evidence that hundreds of people are currently building the game.

"Some form of preproduction probably exists" and "the game is in full production" are very different claims.

## What about Yoshida, Sakaguchi, Kitase, and Nomura?

Yoshida is the easiest case. He has spent years telling us that XVII probably should not simply be his next game. He has never issued a binding declaration that he will have zero involvement, and Square Enix's reorganized structure makes clean team boundaries less useful than they once were. Still, a Yoshida-led repeat of the XVI setup looks less likely than it did before his own comments.

Hironobu Sakaguchi is fun to imagine and unsupported by evidence. His relationship with Square Enix is much warmer than it was for many years, and he remains deeply engaged with the series as a player and elder statesman. There is no credible public indication that he is secretly directing XVII.

Yoshinori Kitase and Tetsuya Nomura are also unknowns. Their involvement would not be surprising in some producer, creative, design, or supervisory capacity, especially if Hamaguchi were involved. It would also be perfectly possible for XVII to be built without either man occupying a central role.

The useful answer for all three is the same: possible is not known.

## The MMO theory

Fans occasionally point out a pleasing pattern:

- *Final Fantasy XI* was an MMO.
- *Final Fantasy XIV* was an MMO.
- Seventeen comes three numbered games later.

There is no evidence that *Final Fantasy XVII* is an MMO.

The pattern is numerology until Square Enix gives us something stronger. *Final Fantasy XIV* also remains an actively supported subscription business with major expansion plans. Square Enix could someday decide that the next era of online Final Fantasy needs another numbered MMO, but operating two giant numbered Final Fantasy MMOs at once would be a major strategic choice. Nothing public currently points to it.

I would assign the MMO theory a low probability.

## The engine tells us nothing yet

There is no credible engine information.

If Hamaguchi's organization develops XVII, Unreal Engine would be an easy guess because the *Remake* trilogy has given the team years of experience with Epic's technology. That does not make Unreal Engine 5 a known fact.

Square Enix could use an internal technology stack, a customized engine derived from another project, a future version of Unreal, or something else entirely.

This is a good example of how rumor chains become fake facts:

Hamaguchi might direct XVII. Hamaguchi's current team uses Unreal. Therefore XVII uses Unreal.

Each step sounds reasonable. Only the middle statement is currently grounded.

## What a multiplatform XVII probably looks like

Of all the speculative sections in this article, platforms require the least imagination.

Square Enix explicitly says it is pursuing a multiplatform strategy for major franchises and AAA games. Its recent Final Fantasy releases are following that policy. A XVII that reaches PlayStation, Xbox, and PC either simultaneously or within a narrow window is therefore much easier to defend than another years-long PlayStation exclusivity period.

Nintendo is harder to predict because XVII's hardware target is unknown. The relevant question may not even be "Can Switch 2 run it?" by the time the game ships. A later Nintendo platform could be in the market, or Square Enix could design the game around a broader hardware floor from the beginning.

So I would separate two claims:

Broad multiplatform support is highly likely.

The exact launch-day hardware list is unknowable.

## Release timing

*Final Fantasy XV* arrived in 2016. *Final Fantasy XVI* followed in 2023. Repeating that seven-year gap would put XVII around 2030.

Square Enix says it wants to improve the cadence of major titles, which argues against blindly projecting the old interval forward. The lack of an announcement in August 2026 argues against expecting the game soon.

My current guess is a reveal in 2027 or 2028 and a release around 2029 or 2030.

A 2028 release is possible if development is already much farther along than the public record suggests. A 2031-or-later date becomes increasingly plausible if the project is still early or if the eventual director cannot fully move onto it until another large game ships.

This is one of the areas where a single credible leak about staffing could move the estimate by years.

## Claims I would currently reject

Until Square Enix or a source with a real track record provides evidence, I would not treat any of the following as known:

- XVII is turn-based.
- XVII is an action game.
- XVII uses *Final Fantasy VII Remake* combat.
- XVII is an MMO.
- XVII is open world.
- XVII has a medieval setting.
- XVII has a science-fiction setting.
- XVII stars a woman, a man, or a customizable protagonist.
- XVII uses Unreal Engine 5.
- XVII is a PlayStation 6 game.
- XVII releases in a particular year.
- Hamaguchi is confirmed as director.
- Creative Studio III has been formally ruled out.
- Sakaguchi is involved.
- Nomura is involved.
- Kitase is involved.
- Nobuo Uematsu is involved.
- Masayoshi Soken is involved.
- Yoshitaka Amano has a defined role beyond whatever series branding work Square Enix may eventually commission.
- Square Enix has finalized the combat system.

Some of these guesses will eventually turn out to be true. That will not make the current evidence stronger in retrospect.

## What I think is happening

My best reconstruction is that Square Enix already has some kind of succession plan for the numbered series, but the public evidence does not tell us how mature it is.

Yoshida spent years arguing that the next team should be free to reinvent Final Fantasy rather than copy XVI. Square Enix then reorganized its Japanese studios, tightened oversight of large projects, committed to multiplatform releases, and told investors it wanted a steadier flow of major games.

At the same time, Hamaguchi's group has become an unusually valuable asset. It is a large, experienced JRPG team that has survived across three consecutive AAA productions instead of dissolving after each release. Its director wants to make another JRPG. He has explicitly named "a new Final Fantasy title" as one possible destination for the team.

Then Yoshida brought up Hamaguchi making XVII on stage.

The simplest version of the theory is therefore easy to state: Square Enix may be preparing Hamaguchi and some version of the *Final Fantasy VII Remake* organization to inherit the next numbered game.

There are still obvious alternatives. XVII may already belong to another internal group. Hamaguchi may be a candidate for XVIII instead. His team's next Final Fantasy may be unnumbered. The Berlin exchange may have been nothing more than two developers having fun with the audience.

For now, I would call Hamaguchi the leading hypothesis rather than the answer.

The most encouraging piece of actual evidence is broader than any one director. Everything Yoshida has said about XVII points toward freedom. The next creators do not seem publicly bound to XVI's combat, tone, world, or production identity. Square Enix appears to want another reinvention.

That leaves almost the entire creative page blank.

For Final Fantasy, that is a pretty good place to start.
