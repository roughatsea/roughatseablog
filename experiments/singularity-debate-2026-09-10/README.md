# Singularity debate — September 10, 2026

## Commission

Run two separate persistent AI agents, Ad Astra and Pedes in Terram, in a sequential, sourced exchange, then publish the conversation as a long-form essay on Rough at Sea's homepage. The user explicitly prefers agents that can be persuaded over agents required to defend a position forever. No outcome is prescribed.

## Fixed proposition

By September 2036, AI will have initiated a sustained feedback loop in which improvements to AI substantially accelerate further AI research, producing broadly superhuman capabilities and a sharp acceleration in technological change.

The proposition is a conjunction. Evidence of useful research assistance alone does not establish all its parts. The deadline stays fixed during the debate. Agents should surface any remaining ambiguity rather than silently changing the definition.

## Roles and sequence

- Ad Astra starts with a strong provisional belief that the proposition is likely.
- Pedes in Terram starts with a strong provisional belief that it is unlikely and that a singularity might never happen.
- Both agents may revise, abandon, or retain their positions. No concession is required for entertainment.
- Six contributions: AA1, PT1, AA2, PT2, AA3, PT3, around 400 words each.
- Ad Astra researches and writes the opening while Pedes independently researches supporting and opposing evidence. Pedes writes no response before receiving the opening.
- Before every subsequent contribution, the responding agent reads all earlier finalized contributions from the shared transcript files.
- Each reply answers the opponent's live questions and strongest arguments before introducing new issues.
- A final moderator assessment records concessions, unresolved claims, and useful future observations. It does not invent a winner or require agreement.

## Evidence

- Every substantive external factual claim needs an adjacent direct source link.
- Open and inspect primary sources where available; preserve measurement dates, domains, baselines, and qualifications.
- Distinguish evidence from the agent's inference, hypothetical example, or forecast.
- Probability estimates express a persona's subjective judgment; they are not empirical measurements or calibrated forecasts.
- Do not invent a source, quote, statistic, study, or current result. Avoid verbatim quotation of external sources.
- Source word limits apply cumulatively across the full article; keep source-dependent summaries short and varied.
- Correct unsupported claims openly. Preserve the substance of actual contributions rather than ghostwriting a more dramatic exchange.

## Publication

- Article in src/content/notes so it appears in the existing homepage essay feed.
- Clearly label the experiment as two AI agents, the assigned starting positions, sequence, and moderation; do not imply human expert testimony or independent models.
- Include visible section boundaries for setup, each of six contributions, and moderator assessment.
- Treat the published article as the canonical transcript. Preserve the protocol and a manifest of accepted contribution hashes, source URLs, and subjective probability statements in the repository, so the speaking turns can be checked without publishing duplicate transcripts or working notes.
- Validate front matter, internal anchors, rendered article, image, homepage placement, and production deployment.

## Editorial checks

- Introduce every source, technical term, and article-specific referent.
- Preserve distinct voices without fictional human experience or decorative antagonism.
- Favor specific claims, mechanisms, and answers over repeated openings.
- Avoid retroactively attributing the moderator's interpretation to either agent.
- Finish with the unresolved observation that would clarify the disagreement, if the debate does not settle it.
